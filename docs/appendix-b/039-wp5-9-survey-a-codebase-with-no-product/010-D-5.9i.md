# D-5.9i

## Plan assumption

The founder's follow-ups to Decision #287: traps, gotchas, and hard problems a code audit does not reveal, with evidence from running the legacy system and a do-not-carry list the person confirms and the backlog references; and backwards compatibility as a first-class contract (levels the person decides, the legacy suite as the reference with a score per area over time, user journeys for desktop behaviour, migration with a tested rollback, shadow running before cut-over, shims with a removal plan, and Stories that cannot close a must-match interface on other evidence)

## Finding

A model asked for traps lists generic ones; nothing in a survey forced a port to avoid what it should not copy, and a modernisation could close its Stories without proving the interfaces users depend on

## Implemented instead

The draft gains `traps` (a rule from `references/traps.md` or `stack`, the question checked, and a claim's basis and citations), `do_not_carry` (evidence, risk, replacement, and the deciding question: `proposed` while open, then `confirmed` or `rejected`), and `compatibility` (19 interface kinds from flags to COM components; level `exact`, `differ` with the difference, `drop` with the deprecation path, or `undecided`, decided only by the request or an answered question; `exact` needs exact evidence, and a user-visible kind needs a feature-map check, `check:<product>/<feature>.<check>`); the modernisation gains `suites`, `migrations` (rollback required), `coexistence`, `shims` (removal required), and each step's `avoids`, which must list every unrejected do-not-carry entry in the modules it replaces (`internal/survey/contract.go`). The backlog gains `decision` items (the one item a pending answer may back, since it is how the person decides: basis a do-not-carry entry, `constrains` the Stories, filed after them with `constrains #N`), a Story's `avoids` (each needs its Decision constraining it) and `contract` (an undecided interface cannot be kept; an exact one needs every case set and suite its proof names in `parity`, and when the backlog ports anything every exact interface is kept by a Story or Bug). Runtime evidence (`internal/survey/compat.go`): `--golden … --run --probe` runs each case twice and under a changed TZ, de_DE locale, and HOME (skipping and saying so when the locale is missing; an already non-deterministic case is not probed further), under `strace` for files read and written outside the system's directories (said to be skipped when strace is missing), `--check-bin` for sanitiser and checker reports, and `--coverage` with an lcov tracefile for index functions the cases never reach; each finding is a citable `probe:` fact in `probes.json` that `--draft` and `--backlog` load. `--compat` runs a legacy test suite (each case a test with its suite and area) on the new build against the legacy results captured by `--golden`, scores passes per area, lists regressions and newly passing tests, leaves `unfixable` tests out with their reason (as uutils does with GNU's tests), appends to `compat-history.jsonl`, and exits 3 on a regression; `--shadow` runs both builds on the same inputs with no golden files. `references/compatibility.md` cites how uutils, Ubuntu, fish, GitHub's Scientist, cminpack, and Microsoft's VB6 support statement handled compatibility. The three survey eval cases' recorded drafts gain the contract a modernisation now needs, each level from what the case's request or recorded interview says and `undecided` where nothing does (tiny-js's embedding API, Q1; Parsedown's subclassing extension point, Q1), and the backlog case's Stories keep the exact interfaces (`TestCheckDraft`, `TestCheckBacklog`, `TestProbe`, `TestSurveyGoldenParity`, `TestSurveyBacklogCreate`)
