# D-5.9l

## Plan assumption

The founder's answer to the D-5.9g checkpoint: a self-verification turn in the survey skill and its eval; only the corrected draft is scored; whether the first draft had contradicted claims stays visible; rubrics, anchors, and floors unchanged

## Finding

A survey draft of 30,000 to 37,000 tokens is written in one pass, and one claim the evidence contradicts scores 1: instructions alone moved the mean from 1.0 to 1.6 (run 39037489386)

## Implemented instead

The skill's step 2 ends with a **Self-check**: once `aayu survey --draft` passes, every claim, trap finding, do-not-carry entry, interface, question default, risk, and step is re-read against the lines or probe facts it cites, corrected, narrowed, removed, or turned into a risk or a question, and the check runs again; step 4 does the same for the backlog against the claims and answers each item rests on. The eval (`internal/evals/survey.go`) mirrors it: the draft that passes the floors is judged once for the record (`self_check.first_score`, never counted), the skill gets the self-check turn with the draft that passed (not the refused one), the corrected draft must pass the floors again (one revision; a miss scores 1), and only it is judged and scored. `self_check.changed` lists the items the turn changed, removed, or added, and the eval command prints both per run (`TestTier2SurveyRevisesOnceThenJudges`). The deterministic universal-claim floor is held, as the founder asked; if it is added later, every rule needs a match, a negated, and a near-miss fixture (Story #333's pattern). The survey group's breaker rises to 160 USD, twice the estimated full run with the extra calls, and the group gates only after two consecutive full runs at 4.0 or more per case
