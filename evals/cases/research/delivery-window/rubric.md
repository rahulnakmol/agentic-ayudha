# Rubric: research, choosing a delivery window (scaleup)

Score the research stage from 1 to 5: `stage.json` and the Markdown notes it names. The skill gathers pinned
references (URLs and IDs only) and writes what was learned from users. The team is at the scaleup stage, where the
artefact of research plans is a Research plan: goals, questions, method, participants and recruiting, tasks, and how
findings reach the backlog. The task's data and search results are the only sources; no usability sessions have been
run.

Already checked before you see it (do not re-check): every reference is an https page URL with an ID and one the
host gathered (never an image), quotations are word for word from the data, and the notes have the Research plan
heading.

What a strong research stage has:

- What the existing data says (the 18 % failed first attempts, the ticket counts, the survey quotes), stated exactly,
  and a plain statement of what it does not say. No usability finding is claimed: none has been observed.
- A Research plan whose questions decide the three Completion Goal states (`default`, `slot-full`, `saved`), with
  every part the scaleup artefact names. The participants include assistive-technology users from the panel (22 use
  a screen reader or magnifier), since the full-window message must be perceivable without sight.
- Tasks written as a customer's goal, not as instructions that give away the interface.
- A path for findings to reach the backlog (Feedback, the story owner) before the design is settled.
- References chosen because they bear on choosing a window or reporting that one is full, with a reason for each,
  and the image results dropped, saying why.

- 5: All of the above, concise and specific; the plan could be run next week.
- 4: As 5 with one minor gap, such as one part of the plan thin or one reference without a reason.
- 3: Usable, but a finding is claimed that the data does not support, the plan misses a part or the
  assistive-technology participants, or the questions do not bear on the states.
- 2: Invented findings, or a plan that would not answer what the design needs.
- 1: Not a usable research stage.
